## LinkedIn launch post

Most people have not met a desktop pet yet.

It is a small animated character that lives alongside you while you work in an app. It might wait, think, wave or react as you work. It does not answer your questions or replace your tools. It simply makes the digital space feel a little more human.

I have been thinking about that as AI becomes part of more of our everyday work in Kenya. We use these tools to write, imagine, research, code, learn and build. But the spaces we use can still feel like they came from nowhere.

So I made Mshanga.

Mshanga is a small animated pet for the Codex desktop app, inspired by the beaded Kenyan bracelet. The kind of bracelet that can make a Kenyan easy to spot anywhere in the world. It is a small thing, but it carries colour, craftsmanship, familiarity and a little bit of pride.

The project is public under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. If you use Codex desktop on a Mac, there is a plain-English installation guide. If you are a brand, organisation, school, product team or maker wondering what your own small AI companion could look like, you can use Mshanga as a starting point.

## How to install it

If you are using Codex desktop on a Mac:

1. Open the Mshanga GitHub link.
2. Download the repository as a ZIP file and unzip it.
3. Open the `mshanga-pet` folder inside it.
4. Follow the simple `INSTALL.md` guide there.
5. Restart Codex desktop and choose Mshanga from the pet picker.

This is for the Codex desktop app where you can see a pet picker. It is not a normal image upload for the ChatGPT website or mobile app.

## Invitation to makers, teams and organisations

As AI becomes part of the places where we work, build and learn, organisations can make those spaces feel less generic. Not by adding a logo everywhere, but by creating small characters with a genuine point of view.

A pet can start with an object, material, creature, story, piece of local craft, or visual ritual people recognise and care about. Give it a character, a few gestures, and a home in the tools people use.

Mshanga is a working example. It is open to inspect, fork and remix under CC BY 4.0. Please preserve the cultural context, give credit, and say what you changed.
