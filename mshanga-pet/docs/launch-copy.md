## The big idea

A pet is a small animated character that lives in the Codex desktop app. It
reacts while you work—waiting, thinking, waving or moving—so an otherwise
quiet interface feels a little more alive. Think of it as a tiny visual
companion, not another chatbot.

## Repository description

An open, animated Kenyan-beadwork-inspired desktop pet for Codex: Mshanga, a
bright-eyed woven cuff with a transparent v2 sprite sheet.

## LinkedIn

Most people have not met a desktop pet yet.

It is a small animated character that lives alongside your work in the Codex
desktop app. It might wait, think, wave or run while you prompt. A tiny detail,
but one that can make a digital workbench feel less anonymous.

I wanted mine to feel like it came from somewhere I know. So I made Mshanga:
a bright-eyed character inspired by the dense, joyful geometry of Kenyan
beaded cuff bracelets. It keeps the black, red, white and green beadwork and
turns it into a companion—never an official symbol, simply an affectionate
piece of visual inspiration.

The finished pet is open source. If you are on Codex for Mac, the installation
guide is written for non-technical people. And if you lead a team, a brand or a
community that wants a more distinctive AI workspace, you can use the project
as a starting point for your own pet.

## Invitation for organisations and makers

Could your AI workspace have a mascot with a point of view?

Mshanga is a working example of how a cultural or brand cue can become a small,
useful character rather than a flat logo: define a recognisable silhouette,
give it a visual story, create a handful of emotional behaviours, and package
it so people can install it easily.

If you want to create a pet for your organisation, product or community, start
by choosing an ownable object, textile, creature or symbol; gather reference
images; decide what it should feel like; then adapt the animation sheet and
metadata in this project. Keep the source culture and contributors visible.
